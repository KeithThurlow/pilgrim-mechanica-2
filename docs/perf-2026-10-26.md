# Where the frame goes: stress lab, 2026-10-26

A one-off measurement (Keith 2026-10-26: "just to see where we're at now, but not something we repeatedly run"). The
stress lab (`labs/stress/`) puts every combat system on one battlefield at scale; Run ramps the counts to 25, 50, 100
and 150% and prints average and worst ms per stage. **These numbers are from the cloud box: software rendering
(SwiftShader), shared with other agents' browsers, so they are pessimistic, several times Keith's PC.** Read them for
proportions, not for the budget. Keith's run on his PC gives the real numbers: open the lab, press Run, wait about
40 s, copy the table (it also lands in the console) into this file.

## The setup

- 100%: 1,000 robots (the share sliders give about 820 small, 150 medium, 35 big), 500 bandits (4% rocketeers), 18 war
  band vehicles and 2 bandit mechs, all topped up as they die, spawning 70 m out; the player's Rampart walking slow
  loops, both miniguns on the nearest enemy, can't die. 150% caps robots at 1,000 (the swarm's slots).
- A barrage every 4 s: 10 missile blasts over 0.6 s round the nearest enemy 15 m+ out, then a big mortar blast, all
  through `Battlefield.blast` (dust, debris, sound, splash on everything, a queued crater each).
- 30% of enemy rounds draw a tracer; all of them resolve.
- Each step settles 3 s, then records 6 s (183 frames at a fixed 0.033 s step).

## What to fix first (ranked by what it buys at these counts)

1. **The dust's ground lookups** (dust 72-108 ms headless, about 60% of the CPU frame). `DustDraw.update` asks the full
   height function 3 times plus a normal for every puff's shadow, every frame, and the sim once more. Cache each puff's
   ground (height and normal) when it spawns and refresh it only when the puff has drifted a metre or so, or every Nth
   frame per puff as the sun march already does. Expected: tens of ms headless, a few ms on Keith's PC. Small change,
   one file (`src/fx/dustDraw.ts`, plus `dust.ts`); the ground-dent agent is in `src/fx/` now, so after it merges.
2. **A cheap height for looks** (the other ~15 ms of ground calls: robot legs, swarm sims, cars, deaths floor). The
   chunks already hold the heights in their vertices; a `field.heightCached(x, z)` reading a sampled grid round the
   camera (bilinear) would serve everything that draws, leaving the exact function to feet, rounds and physics. The
   crater layer inside `sample` (about 9 ms) is being reworked by the ground-dent agent (GPU dent layer) already.
3. **Triangles and the shadow pass**: bandits 2,272 tris each: a 300-500 tri LOD past 30-40 m and shadows only from
   the nearest 100 would take 2.3M down to well under 0.5M. Robots: fine per triangle, but 13k parts cast shadows;
   small ones could skip the shadow pass. Terrain chunks cast shadows over the whole streamed radius; only the near
   ring needs to. Measure on Keith's PC first: if his GPU time is low, leave it.
4. **Robots draw** (12-14 ms headless): set instance colours only when they change (`Color.setHex` per instance per
   frame now), and pose the small ones' legs from the sim's own foot positions rather than asking the ground again.
5. **Deaths physics** (7-11 ms with 275-625 bodies, worst frames 56-685 ms when many die at once): lower
   `maxRagdolls` for this many, or let small robots and far bandits skip the ragdoll (a canned fall) past a count.
   Spread new ragdolls over frames to kill the spikes.
6. **War band** (3-7 ms for 18-27 vehicles): per-wheel ground samples and O(n²) avoidance; small now, folds into the
   register (`docs/proposal-register.md`, step 6).
7. **Garbage** (4 ms of GC a frame): the per-call target lists (an object per enemy), `new Vector3` per turret threat,
   the missile swarm rebuilding the target list per missile. The register proposal removes these.

The swarm sims themselves are not the problem: 1,000 robots in 4 ms and 750 bandits in 6 ms headless, both with their
own spatial hashes. The register's cost at these counts is 0.16-0.28 ms a frame (`docs/proposal-register.md`); it is a
structural change, not a speed fix.

### What changed, fix by fix

1. **Dust's ground lookups.** A cheap ground for looks, `src/world/chunkGround.ts`: heights and normals off the streamed
   chunks' own triangles (exactly the drawn surface; true ground where no chunk is loaded). Node: height 2.5 µs to
   0.5 µs, normal 10.3 µs to 0.6 µs, a sun march 44 µs to 5 µs; 0.5 cm from the true ground on average, 13 cm at worst
   (crests between vertices). On top, every puff keeps the ground under it and under its shadow as a plane (height and
   slope) and reads it again only after drifting `dustGroundRefresh` (1.5 m) or on its turn every `dustGroundEvery`
   (30) frames; the dune-shade march runs every `dustSunEvery` (8) frames per puff (was 4). Dust and small robots'
   legs read the cheap ground; feet, rounds, physics and AI keep the true one.
2. **Dent patch shadows.** The patch (295k triangles) only catches shadows now (`shadowTerrain` on). Spacing and window
   were already sliders (`patchSpacing`, `dentCoverage`); see the shots below for 0.25 vs 0.5 m.
3. **Terrain in the shadow pass.** Measured at the stress lab's start: 1,075k triangles in the shadow pass, of which
   the patch 295k and the chunks 90k (11 of the 69 chunks fall in the sun's box): **36% of the shadow pass was
   terrain**, so the coarse copy was worth it. Each chunk now carries a shadow-only copy (every `shadowSpacing` 4 m,
   512 triangles, sunk `shadowSink` 0.3 m so it can't poke through hollows as acne), on a layer only the sun's shadow
   camera sees (`SHADOW_ONLY_LAYER`); the drawn chunks and the patch only catch. Shadow pass: 1,075k to 786k
   triangles; terrain 385k to 6k. Craters patch the copy along with the chunk. `shadowTerrain` 0 puts the old way back.
4. **Robots drawing.** Paint colours worked out once per hex (no `setHex` per part per frame) and written to the
   instance buffer only where they changed, uploaded only then; small robots' legs on the cheap ground.
5. **Ragdoll spikes.** At most `ragdollsPerFrame` (6) new ragdolls get their physics in a frame; the rest fly on as
   thrown (ballistic, not below where they died) and are made over the next frames. The cap on moving bodies
   (`maxRagdolls`) was already a slider. Node, a barrage killing 30 robots and 30 bandits in one frame: worst physics
   frame 56 / 121 / 56 ms to 15 / 26 / 13 ms (three runs), median frame 7.3 to 5.8 ms.

## Pass 2 (2026-10-26, agent): the scale build

Keith approved `docs/proposal-scale.md` ("we just have to do it, and then I can see it"). Built in the proposal's order
on `wip/scale` (handoff `docs/handoff-scale.md`): the effects budget, shots as data with the Protocols as fields (and
real Protocol behaviours replacing the worst case's stand-ins), then GPU particles. Every new value is a slider
(Performance, Effects, and a Protocols group in the weapons topic).

### What changed

1. **Effects budget** (`src/fx/budget.ts`): every dust source and minigun impact asks one gate. Yours stay full (your
   own rounds' hits anywhere, anything within 15 m of your mech); others' hits in one 1.5 m cell in a frame merge;
   past 30 m of the camera focus a falling share is kept (30% at 90 m), evenly spread; under pressure (dust asked a
   frame, live dust against the pool) far effects thin harder and the extras go in rank order.
2. **Shots as data** (`src/game/shots.ts`): `Battlefield.fire` is a seeded command; rows resolve once a frame through
   the register (plain rays now culled like the sheet already was), hits and the rest as plain-data events, draws to
   the effects. Protocols are fields (split, fire rate, area, ricochet, chain) and come from five new Protocol items.
   The worst case passes its max Protocols as fields instead of calling `fire` again per stand-in.
3. **GPU particles** (`src/fx/gpu*.ts`): sparks, grit, casings, tracers and dust on TSL compute rings (spawn rows in a
   float texture, so the same code runs on WebGPU and on WebGL 2's transform feedback), drawn straight off the
   buffers. Pools: dust 32,768 (CPU 4,096), sparks and grit 8,192 (CPU 600), casings 2,048 (240), tracers 1,024 (160).

What it says:
- **The budget** cuts what the worst case asks for by about 60% (1,584 to 596 dust puffs a frame) while your own
  effects are untouched; on the CPU pool that still leaves the pool full (pressure 2), which is why the GPU pool exists.
- **GPU dust** takes the dust off the CPU (3.9 to 0.34 ms in the worst case: what's left is writing the spawn rows and
  the live estimate) and holds 5x the puffs alive (22k, pool 32k), so trails no longer blink out. The GPU's own cost
  can't be read on this box (SwiftShader runs it on the same two cores); on Keith's PC it is one compute dispatch of
  32k particles and 2 x 32k sprites plus 32k shadow quads, mostly overdraw. The live count and the per-frame budget
  (`fxGpuDustPerFrame` 1,600) are the knobs if the far zoom gets heavy.
- **Shots as data** costs about the same as the old calls (1.5-2.2 ms for every team's rounds) and the worst case's
  Protocol rounds are now real ones (split, rate, area, ricochet, chain from the shot's own fields).
- This worst-case run fired fewer team rounds than the proposal's measurement (about 1 gun shot a frame against 2-4);
  the allies' aim and arcs decide it. The proposal's 32-54 `fire` calls a frame would be 32-54 commands here.
